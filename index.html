    Questions

    Is it safe to generate a password on a website?

    Here, yes. Every value is created on your device by your browser's cryptographic random number generator and never leaves it. The page has no analytics, cookies or third-party scripts, and its security policy blocks it from connecting to any server. You can check by turning off your internet connection, or by reading the source code.

    Are my passwords saved anywhere?

    No. Only your settings are remembered, in this browser. Values you copy are listed under "Copied this session" until you close or reload the page, and are never written to storage.

    Does it work offline?

    Yes. After your first visit your browser keeps a copy of the page, so it works without a connection. You can also install it as an app: use "Add to Home Screen" on your phone, or the install icon in your browser's address bar.

    Should I use a password or a passphrase?

    Use a random password for accounts saved in a password manager: it packs the most strength into each character. Use a passphrase for things you have to remember or type, like your password manager's master password or your computer login.

    How long should my password be?

    A random password of 16 or more characters, or a passphrase of 5 or more words, is strong for almost anything. If a site limits length or symbols, change the options to match its rules; the strength meter shows how much protection you keep.

    How is strength calculated?

    From how the value was generated, not how it looks: how many characters or words were possible and how many were picked, measured in bits. Crack times assume an attacker trying 10 billion guesses per second against a stolen password database.

    Is it free?

    Yes, with no ads, accounts or limits. It's open source on GitHub.
